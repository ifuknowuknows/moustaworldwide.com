The vision

Same same, but different.

Wherever I go, people want the same things: to belong, to be safe, to be taken seriously. What changes is how each culture says it. When we forget that, we stop seeing each other as people.

I've seen where that leads. In Iraq I interviewed Syrian refugees, and in Rwanda survivors of the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi. So I build things that put people in the same room.

Why that matters

Understanding people on their terms, not mine.

A language carries far more than words. It holds a culture's humour, its history, its codes of politeness, its biases and the things it never needs to say out loud.

When I speak with someone in their mother tongue, I'm running on their operating system instead of my own. I hear the joke the way it was meant. I notice the assumptions built into the language, and the ones built into mine.

That's where real connection starts. Learning a language is learning a way of seeing the world. Perspective is everything.

Writing

AI literacy

AI will change the world. Everyone should understand it.

I've spent years nerding out on AI: how it works, what it can do, and how it can be used for us and against us.

The world is on the cusp of massive, irreversible change. Some of it for good, some of it for bad. Right now, understanding AI is a privilege. I want to make it accessible to everyone, in plain words, because all of us need that literacy to have a say in what comes next.
